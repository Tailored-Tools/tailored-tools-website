Why customers who find you through AI are different

17 February 2026 · 3 min read · by James Edwards

A study by SE Ranking found that customers who arrive at a website via ChatGPT convert at around 15.9%. Google organic traffic converts at around 1.76%. That's nearly ten times higher. The number is striking enough that it's worth understanding why it happens, because it changes how you should think about AI credibility.

AI does the shortlisting for them

When someone Googles "therapist in Bristol," they get a list of ten results and browse a few. They might spend an hour comparing options, reading reviews, checking prices. By the time they contact you, they're still early in the decision process. They've chosen to look at your website but haven't necessarily chosen you.

When someone asks an AI assistant for a recommendation, the AI has already done the comparison. It's given them a shortlist of two or three options that match their criteria. The person who then contacts you has already passed through a filter. They've been told you're a reasonable match for what they need. The decision is mostly made before they visit your site.

Higher intent, less browsing

AI-referred visitors tend to arrive with higher intent and spend less time on a site before making contact. They're not there to browse or compare. They've already decided they want this kind of service. They're checking you're legitimate before getting in touch. A clear, professional site that quickly confirms you do what the AI said you do is usually enough. The selling was done before they arrived.

The flip side: if AI doesn't recommend you, you don't exist

For Google search, you can appear anywhere from position one to a hundred. People occasionally scroll past the top results. For AI recommendations, you either get mentioned or you don't. There's no page two. If AI doesn't have enough information to recommend you confidently, it picks someone else, and you never knew the opportunity existed. The conversion rate is high, but the cost of being absent is total.

The audience is smaller but faster-growing

AI search still represents a small fraction of total web traffic, roughly 1% as of early 2025. But it grew 527% between January and May 2025 alone. The businesses building AI credibility now are not chasing a huge audience. They're establishing a presence in a channel that converts well and is growing fast, before it becomes as competitive as Google organic already is. Early credibility in a growing channel is consistently more valuable than late entry once competition has driven up the difficulty.

The practical implication

You don't need to choose between Google SEO and AI credibility. They share many of the same foundations: a credible website, consistent listings, active reviews, and clear service information. But the businesses that treat them as identical will leave gaps. AI needs specific signals that Google doesn't require in the same way, particularly structured data and explicit pricing signals.
