Where it started

Square was fighting me.

I was tired of Square’s awkward interface, and of having no proper control from my phone. So I replaced Square, Weebly, Carrd, my spreadsheets and every hand-typed message with one system I built myself.

I call it SS OS. Keep scrolling and watch the six become one.

Every night

While I sleep, eight jobs do themselves.

A nightly maintenance run works through eight steps in order. By morning, everything is up to date.

  1. 01Marks past events
  2. 02Expires passes
  3. 03Emails pass-expiry reminders
  4. 04Generates follow-up nudges
  5. 05Updates stats
  6. 06Sends capacity alerts
  7. 07Spots no-shows
  8. 08Clears stale orders

Underneath

Calm on the surface. Serious engineering underneath.

The parts nobody sees. They join up as you scroll, the way they do in the system: through the same data.

One set of data, two front doors

The public website is 35 pages, including local pages for sound baths in Maidstone and Dartford and aerial sound baths in Kent. It reads from the same data as the OS, so an event appears on the site the moment it exists.

A proper checkout

Stripe checkout, with vouchers and gift cards in one basket.

Bookings that bend

A booking can be moved, parked or cancelled. A refund made inside the OS reverses the revenue, restores pass credits and updates the customer’s record, all in one go.

Rhythm Club

A children’s after-school drumming club, launched on the same booking engine. Consent forms, named collectors and a collection password are built in.

A community of 1,259

People across WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram. On WhatsApp, messages go out from pre-approved templates instead of being typed one by one.

Where everything went

Six old tools, and what replaced them.

  1. Square

    Stripe checkout, and bookings I can move, park, cancel or refund from my phone.

  2. Weebly & Carrd

    A 35-page website that reads the same data as the OS.

  3. Spreadsheets

    Customer records in the OS, with passes and stats kept current by the nightly run.

  4. Hand-typed WhatsApps

    Pre-approved templates, prepared automatically, sent with my yes.

  5. Hand-typed emails

    Pass-expiry reminders emailed every night, and an inbox that sorts itself into five piles.
