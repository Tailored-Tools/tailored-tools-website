A website that breathes like her class.

Jasmine Knight teaches yoga, breathwork and meditation for the people who look after everyone else. She'd been wary of website builders, but workplaces were starting to ask about her sessions, and she needed somewhere that felt like her to send them.

Before any code

I didn't start with a template. I started with her.

Every touch on her site comes from something I noticed about Jasmine, her name, and the people she teaches.

I noticed

Her name is a bedtime.

Knight. Goodnight. She'd always loved the pun.

So I made

The whole site a night sky, with her emblem redrawn as a constellation. It's the one drawing itself behind these words as you scroll.

I noticed

She notices you.

Meeting Jasmine, you feel looked after before she's said a word.

So I made

A moon that leans towards your cursor as you explore, glowing a little as you get close. On a phone, it drifts by itself.

Try it: move your cursor here
I noticed

She teaches stillness.

Her classes slow people down. A busy website would undo that.

So I made

A breathing ring that paces you to four in, four held, six out, the same rhythm as her guided breathwork. It only breathes while you can see it.

I noticed

Her people give more than they get.

Caregivers, mums, teachers, the ones who pour.

So I made

Each of them a constellation of their own. Real ones, chosen for meaning.

Her four constellations
A pause, the way her site does it
in

Most websites shout. Hers asks you to breathe first. Visitors arrive at a class already a little calmer than they were.

Underneath the calm

It does the practical work too.

A beautiful sky is no use if booking is a faff. Each of these joins up as you scroll, like the stars they sit on.

Every way in

Classes, 1:1s and breathwork, corporate sessions and retreats, each with its own page, times and prices.

Health forms, done properly

A proper pre-class health questionnaire, with GP consent only when it's needed. A QR code with her emblem at the centre takes people straight to it.

Consent before anything personal

The 1:1 enquiry asks permission before it asks about anyone's health, the way it should.

She edits it herself

Retreat details, class prices and times, and a homepage announcement: fifteen things she can change in seconds. The design stays safe, and every save pings me so I can check it.

Two teachers, one list

Classes with Hazel, her nan, sit beside her own. Every enquiry routes through one mailing list, so nobody's personal number goes public.

Everything in her name

She registered goodknightyoga.co.uk herself, in her own account, with a step-by-step guide written just for her.

How it went

From new moon to full.

  1. Sunday evening

    Twelve answers: her services, her story, "calm, warm and playful", and a couple of websites she loved.

  2. Within a day

    A live site with her name on it. Her story near word for word, her emblem as a constellation, working forms, and clearly marked spaces for everything still to come.

  3. The next morning

    Her first notes arrive through the site's own feedback button. All of them live by that evening.

  4. Seventeen days

    Five rounds of her feedback, each one live the same day: Hazel's classes, retreats, the corporate page, the health form.

  5. 7 August

    goodknightyoga.co.uk goes public. A full moon.
